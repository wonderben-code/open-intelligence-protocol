First version, in parts as M6 builds them.

- `Lookup`: resolves a mind from the resolvers the client trusts, with the witness rule of core 9.4 applied: each event's time comes from a resolver's record of first sight, and each resolver's copy of a log is verified before it is held. Two resolvers that hold different events for one position show the mind's duplicity. A collective's log is verified with its members' logs, fetched first.
- `exchange`, `askRecord`, `askJson`, `TransportError`: one request to a service over HTTPS, bounded in size and in time, with no redirect followed. A problem of core 10.2 is thrown as an `OipError` with the service's code.
- `Mind`: a mind of one's own, with its keys in a keystore. `create` and `open`; `sign`; and the identity changes `update`, `addKey`, `revokeKey`, `fork` and `retire`, each made in an order that survives a process that stops: the control keys are bound to the event, the event is signed and verified before it leaves, published, read back from a resolver, and only then are the keys that signed it destroyed (Layer 1, 3.11 and 7.4).
- `Mind.publishLocation`, `publishProfile`, `publishRecord`, `bringHomes`: what a mind publishes of itself. A location record (Layer 1, Section 15) and a profile (16) go to every resolver and every home, with requests the mind signs (13.3); a record is put on the mind's feed at its homes (10). An entry is remembered before it leaves, so that a mind signs one entry for a position. `publications` says what each service answered. Every identity event is brought to the mind's homes, the event that retires it among them.
- `Lookup.feed`: reads a mind's feed from every home it names and verifies it; `Resolved` has the mind's current location record, whether it is stale, and its current profile.
- `MindOptions.memory`: where the client remembers its own work between runs, in a storage of `@oip/keystore`.
- `Lookup.find`: asks the indexers the client names a capability query (Layer 1, Section 18), and fetches and verifies every record they name before it returns it (18.4). An answer is taken if the indexer signed it for the query asked. What was named and does not check out is told, with why. `Lookup.record` fetches a record a mind's homes serve, by its content ID. A mind that publishes its profile or its location submits them to the indexers it names.
- `Lookup.aliases`: checks the other names a mind lists (Layer 1, 5.3; Layer 0, Section 4). An alias is the mind's only if it points back: an HTTPS origin, a DNS name, another DID (`did:oip` and `did:web` are resolved here, others by a resolver the application gives), or an AT Protocol handle. The DID document of a resolved mind has a service for each endpoint of its current location record (5.2).
- `Mind.send`, `Mind.receive`: packets (Layer 1, Section 12). `send` signs a packet, seals it to each recipient unless told not to (12.6), and posts it to the first endpoint of the recipient's current location record that takes it, a mailbox last (15.3). An acknowledgement is kept if it is the recipient's own for the packet (13.2). A packet may also be for whoever a query finds, through the indexers the client names (18.5). `receive` reads the mind's mailboxes with requests it signs (13.5), judges each item as 12.4 has a receiver judge it, by the sender's log as it stands after the packet is first seen (core 9.4), and returns each packet once.
- `Mind.moveHomes`: changes the hosts that serve a mind (Layer 1, 15.4). Each new host is first brought the mind's log, location record, profile and whole feed. An `Update` then names the new homes and, with `revoke`, removes a key the host that is left held, so that what that host signs counts no longer. A location record gives the endpoints at the new homes.
- Delegation and the Mind Web: `Mind.delegate`, `subDelegate` and `revoke` (Layer 1, Section 11); `intent`, `offer`, `commitment`, `receipt` and `cosign`, each for the mind itself or, with a chain of delegations, for the mind it acts for (Layer 2, Sections 4 to 7); `countsFor` and `statusOf`, computed from the records the mind holds and the logs of the minds they name, fetched afresh (7 and 8; core 9.4); `hold` and `records`. A packet may be sent for another mind under `/oip/send`, and `receive` says which packets were.
- Collectives (Layer 1, Section 9): `Mind.foundCollective` and `changeCollective` draft a collective's identity events, `signEvent` adds this mind's proof as a member, with an invocation key that signs one event for each position, and `publishLog` verifies and publishes the log. A record is the collective's once the threshold of its members has signed it. `Mind.charter` writes a charter (Layer 2, 6.2), and a member with a role signs for the collective by passing `{ charter }`.
- The client runs in a browser as it is: the scenario of the papers is run in a page of Chromium, with the mind's keys sealed in IndexedDB (ADR-0022).
- Sessions (Layer 1, Section 14): `Mind.openSession`, `send` with `session`, `resume`, `sessions` and `leaveSession`. A packet of a session has its place in the mind's chain before it leaves, and is kept to be sent again. `receive` returns the packets of each chain in order, leaves one that is ahead of a gap in the mailbox, asks for what is missing with a `Resume`, answers a participant's `Resume`, and says in `Inbox.sessions` what it found: gaps, equivocation, what was sent again. `MindOptions.instance` names a client among several that run as one mind. A packet that states no `expires` is taken for seven days.
- `Mind.listen`: a socket held open to the mind's own host (Layer 1, 13.4). The host's challenge is answered with a `Hello` signed for the origin the client connected to (13.3). What the host sends is judged as `receive` judges a mailbox, and the mind says it has a record with an acknowledgement of its own over the socket. A connection that ends is opened again after a wait that doubles, with jitter. `MindOptions.webSocket` and `sleep` are the platform's socket and timer.
- A reading that could not write the client's memory no longer leaves its packets remembered in the process: they are judged again by the next reading, where before they were taken for duplicates and removed unreturned. Two processes of one mind that read at once now merge what each accepted, and each packet is returned by one of them.
- The outbox: a packet that no endpoint of a recipient took, for a reason that may pass, is kept in the client's memory and tried again, by `receive` and `listen` when it is due and by `Mind.retry` at once. `Delivery.queued` says a packet is kept, `Message.queue` says whether to, `Mind.outbox` lists what waits and `Mind.abandon` gives one up. `Inbox.retried` says what came of each try. A reading now judges the packets of one sending chain in the order of the chain, so that the first of a session is not refused for arriving after the others.
- `Mind.close` stops what the mind listens to. The public interface, as `etc/client.api.md` has it, is frozen for 0.x.
- `NewMind.parents`: a mind may be made as a fork of another (Layer 1, 8.1). `Mind.fork` on the parent acknowledges it (8.2).
- `Mind.sightings` and a second argument of `Mind.hold`: the client's own record of when it first obtained each proof of a record (core 9.4, item 1), to keep with the record between runs. Until now a record held again in a later run was judged as if it had only then arrived, so a signature made under a delegation that had since expired, or been revoked, stopped counting when the program restarted.
- `Mind.publishRecord` submits an Offer or an Intent of the mind's own to the indexers the client names, once a home serves it (Layer 1, 18.1), so that `Lookup.find` can find it. `publications` says what each indexer answered. Until now only a profile was submitted.
